# Community Marketing playbook

## Establish the decision

Member job, audience, existing gathering places, moderation capacity, platform constraints and desired business outcome.

Use supplied, authorized evidence first. Label missing inputs and choose a reversible recommendation if uncertainty remains; do not invent a baseline, market statistic or guaranteed improvement.

## Method and tradeoffs

Choose support, practice-sharing, professional identity or product feedback as the primary member value. Start where participants already gather and establish moderation, conduct and escalation rules. Seed useful recurring rituals with real contributors. Define member consent and escalation for harassment, health claims or confidential disclosures; avoid manufactured engagement.

## Worked example

A salon software user group can share scheduling practices and monthly workflow clinics; do not expose customer appointment data or create fake member activity.

## Verify and diagnose

Measure activated contributors, response quality, returning members, resolution time and moderation burden. Compare community-exposed retention cautiously because selection bias is likely.

If evidence is incomplete, state exactly which conclusion cannot be supported and what observation would resolve it. Check for instrumentation faults, cohort selection, seasonality and delayed outcomes before attributing a change to an intervention. Keep unrelated workflows unloaded.

## Deliverable

Provide the artifact requested, its evidence and assumptions, a prioritized recommendation with the material tradeoff, and observable acceptance criteria. Include a staged rollout or recovery plan only when implementation risk requires it. Do not label a proposal as an executed result.
