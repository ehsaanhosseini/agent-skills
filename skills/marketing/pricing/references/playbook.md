# Pricing playbook

## Establish the decision

Customer segments, value delivered, costs, current usage, competition, willingness-to-pay evidence and contractual constraints.

Use supplied, authorized evidence first. Label missing inputs and choose a reversible recommendation if uncertainty remains; do not invent a baseline, market statistic or guaranteed improvement.

## Method and tradeoffs

Compare value metrics for customer comprehension, alignment with benefit, predictability and margin. Choose flat, seat, usage, tier, credit, outcome or hybrid pricing based on those tradeoffs. Separate packaging research from demand estimation: Van Westendorp measures perceived acceptability; MaxDiff informs relative preference. Test candidate prices against actual purchase behavior and retention before rollout.

## Worked example

For PMS software, compare per-property and per-room metrics against customer value and seasonal usage. Model small/large properties, seasonal peaks, implementation costs and support before proposing tiers.

## Verify and diagnose

Show cost and usage scenarios, included limits, overage behavior and gross-margin sensitivity. Define new-customer pilot criteria, notice/migration policy and conversion, net revenue, churn and refund guardrails.

If evidence is incomplete, state exactly which conclusion cannot be supported and what observation would resolve it. Check for instrumentation faults, cohort selection, seasonality and delayed outcomes before attributing a change to an intervention. Keep unrelated workflows unloaded.

## Deliverable

Provide the artifact requested, its evidence and assumptions, a prioritized recommendation with the material tradeoff, and observable acceptance criteria. Include a staged rollout or recovery plan only when implementation risk requires it. Do not label a proposal as an executed result.
