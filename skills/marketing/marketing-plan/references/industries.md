# Cross-industry planning distinctions

| Business | Acquisition/activation outcome | Relevant constraints and guardrails |
|---|---|---|
| SaaS/PMS | Qualified demo or self-serve signup -> first useful account outcome -> retained paid account | Buying group, implementation time, imports, permissions, support and net retention |
| Marketplace | Qualified supply/demand -> fulfilled first match -> repeat transaction | Local liquidity, trust/safety, supply capacity, cancellations and take-rate margin |
| E-commerce | Qualified product interest -> fulfilled paid order -> repeat purchase | Inventory, variants, shipping, returns, refunds, product claims and contribution margin |
| Hotel/hospitality | Guest direct booking and stay, or operator software adoption: clarify which | Seasonality, available room nights, distribution costs, cancellations, privacy and channel mix |
| Healthcare | Appropriate inquiry or appointment, subject to eligibility | No diagnoses in ad/analytics payloads; clinical claims, consent, sensitive targeting and provider capacity |
| Beauty/wellness | Suitable booking -> completed service -> appropriate repeat booking | Honest claims, appointment capacity, no-show economics, sensitive client data |
| Automotive | Qualified inquiry/test drive/service appointment -> completed transaction | Inventory accuracy, financing disclosures, regional rules and long conversion lag |
| Professional/local services | Qualified consultation -> scoped contract -> delivered value | Capacity, real service geography, regulated advice, confidential client facts and long sales cycle |

Use the appropriate metric grain and economics. Do not copy a SaaS trial funnel into a clinic or interpret hotel room occupancy as PMS customer adoption. Treat industry examples as prompts for discovery, not business rules. Preserve public brand positioning and project-specific requirements.
