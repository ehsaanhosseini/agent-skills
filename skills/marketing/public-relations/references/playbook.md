# Public Relations playbook

## Establish the decision

Verifiable news, audience, public evidence, spokesperson, approval chain and confidential boundaries.

Use supplied, authorized evidence first. Label missing inputs and choose a reversible recommendation if uncertainty remains; do not invent a baseline, market statistic or guaranteed improvement.

## Method and tradeoffs

Find an actual newsworthy angle and match it to the publication's audience and recent coverage. Prepare a concise evidence-backed pitch, supporting facts and spokesperson availability. For incidents, separate known facts, uncertainty, actions and next update; coordinate with the incident owner and avoid speculation.

## Worked example

A product launch pitch can explain a documented workflow improvement and offer a real demonstration; an incident statement should not claim root cause before investigation confirms it.

## Verify and diagnose

Fact-check names, dates, statistics, quotes and commitments; review legal/privacy implications and approved disclosures. Measure relevant coverage and qualified impact without promising placement.

If evidence is incomplete, state exactly which conclusion cannot be supported and what observation would resolve it. Check for instrumentation faults, cohort selection, seasonality and delayed outcomes before attributing a change to an intervention. Keep unrelated workflows unloaded.

## Deliverable

Provide the artifact requested, its evidence and assumptions, a prioritized recommendation with the material tradeoff, and observable acceptance criteria. Include a staged rollout or recovery plan only when implementation risk requires it. Do not label a proposal as an executed result.
